ABCDE MARITIME MEDICAL ASSESSMENT GUIDELINES
Centre for Maritime Health Services - Clinical Reference

=== A - AIRWAY ===
The airway must be assessed first in any emergency.
- Check if the airway is clear and unobstructed
- Look for signs of obstruction: noisy breathing, stridor, cyanosis
- If airway is blocked: perform jaw lift, apply suction, insert Guedel airway
- Suspected neck/spine injury: immobilize before airway maneuvers
- Administer oxygen via nasal cannula (up to 5 L/min) or Hudson mask (above 10 L/min)
- If patient is not breathing: initiate CPR immediately
- Document time CPR was initiated

=== B - BREATHING ===
Assess breathing after airway is secured.
- Normal breathing rate: 12-16 breaths per minute
- Below 12: bradypnoea - may indicate CNS depression or fatigue
- Above 20: tachypnoea - may indicate pain, anxiety, hypoxia, or infection
- Above 30: severe respiratory distress - immediate intervention required
- Normal oxygen saturation: 95-100%
- SpO2 below 95%: supplemental oxygen required
- SpO2 below 90%: critical - immediate high-flow oxygen, consider MEDEVAC
- Breathing patterns: shallow breathing may indicate pain or pneumothorax
- Assess: rate, depth, symmetry, use of accessory muscles

=== C - CIRCULATION ===
Assess circulatory status after breathing is confirmed.
- Normal heart rate: 60-80 beats per minute
- Tachycardia above 100 bpm: may indicate pain, shock, dehydration, infection
- Bradycardia below 60 bpm: may indicate heart block, medication effect, hypothermia
- Normal blood pressure: 120-140 systolic / 60-90 diastolic
- Hypotension systolic below 90: shock - immediate IV access, fluids
- Capillary refill time: normal less than 2 seconds
- Capillary refill above 2 seconds: poor perfusion, consider shock
- Skin color: pale indicates poor perfusion or anemia
- Bluish (cyanosis): indicates hypoxia
- Insert venous cannula if capillary refill above 2 seconds or shock suspected
- Measure pulse at wrist (radial), neck (carotid), or groin (femoral)

=== D - DISABILITY ===
Assess neurological status.
- Level of consciousness scale:
  1 = Awake, alert and well orientated
  2 = Unclear, but responds to questions
  3 = Does not respond to questions but responds to pain stimuli
  4 = Unconscious and unresponsive to pain stimuli
- Level 3 or 4: CRITICAL - contact Radio Medical immediately
- Check for convulsions: may indicate epilepsy, head injury, hypoglycemia
- Check for paralysis: may indicate spinal injury or stroke
- Pupil reaction: normal is equal and reactive to light (documented as +/+)
- Unequal pupils or non-reactive: may indicate serious head injury or stroke
- Document exact Glasgow Coma Scale if possible

=== E - EXPOSE ===
Full body examination.
- Perform top-to-toe examination in all trauma cases
- Normal oral temperature: 36.5 degrees Celsius
- Hypothermia: below 35 degrees - warm patient, avoid rough handling
- Fever: above 38 degrees - assess for infection source
- Hyperthermia: above 40 degrees - active cooling required
- Document all visible injuries: lacerations, bruising, swelling, deformity
- Check for signs of internal bleeding: abdominal rigidity, distension
- Preserve patient dignity during examination
