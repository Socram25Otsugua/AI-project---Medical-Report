DANISH MARITIME AUTHORITY - MEDICINE CHEST MFAG A1
Official Mandatory Medicine List for Category A Ships
Version 3.6 April 2026

IMPORTANT: All medicines are referenced by their group number (e.g. 3.1 Paracetamol).
Always reference medicines by their number when advising treatment.
Always advise to consult Radio Medical before administering any medicine.

=== GROUP 1 - OXYGEN ===
1.1+1.3 Medical Oxygen
Form: Gas in pressurized bottles
Dosage: Unconscious without breathing: 15 litres/min (manual ventilation)
Dosage: Unconscious with breathing: 9 litres/min via oxygen mask
Dosage: Other conditions: 9 litres/min via mask
Storage: Keep away from flammable substances, below 50°C
Note: Consult Radio Medical

=== GROUP 2 - LOCAL ANAESTHETICS ===
2.1 Lidocaine - Injection 10mg/ml
Indication: Local anaesthetic for wound suturing
Dosage: 20-50mg normally, max 200mg (20ml)
Note: Must be used in room with access to Adrenaline (4.1) and resuscitation equipment

2.2 Lidocaine - Gel 20mg/g (2%)
Indication: Local anaesthetic for painful wounds, burn cleaning
Dosage: Apply thick layer, cover airtight for 15 minutes
Note: Consult Radio Medical

=== GROUP 3 - ANALGESICS (PAIN RELIEF) ===
3.1 Paracetamol - Tablets 500mg
Indication: Fever, mild pain, headache, muscle pain, joint pain, back pain
Dosage: Adults 1g (2 tablets) 3-4 times daily, max 4g per day
Side effects: Doses above 15-20g cause liver damage
Note: No consultation needed for standard dosing

3.2 Ibuprofen - Tablets 400mg
Indication: Moderate pain, period pain, joint pain, bone pain, fractures, muscle pain
Dosage: 400mg 3-4 times daily, max 1800mg per day
Contraindications: Aspirin allergy, asthma, blood thinners, stomach ulcer symptoms
Note: Consult Radio Medical

3.4 Morphine - Injection 10mg/ml, ampoules 1ml (SEDATIVE - marked Delta)
Indication: Severe pain, major wounds, injuries, burns, fractures, kidney stones, gallstones, heart attack
Dosage: Subcutaneous/intramuscular: 10mg. Intravenous: 5mg slowly
Side effects: Restricts breathing, nausea, vomiting, constipation
Antidote: Naloxone (5.1)
Note: Consult Radio Medical - MUST observe breathing after administration

3.7 Diclofenac - Gel 11.6mg/g
Indication: Skin inflammation, arthritis, pain
Dosage: Apply thin layer 3-4 times daily to intact skin only
Note: Consult Radio Medical

3.8 Diclofenac - Suppositories 50mg
Indication: Kidney stone, gallstone, joint pain
Dosage: 50-100mg once daily
Note: Consult Radio Medical

3.9 Morphine Hydrochloride - Tablets 10mg (SEDATIVE)
Indication: Severe pain
Dosage: 10-30mg every 4 hours
Note: Consult Radio Medical

=== GROUP 4 - ALLERGY, SHOCK AND SEASICKNESS ===
4.1 Epinephrine (Adrenaline) - Injection 1mg/ml, ampoules 1ml
Indication: Allergic shock (anaphylaxis)
Dosage: 0.6mg (half ampoule) deep intramuscular immediately
WARNING: Potentially fatal if used for shock caused by blood or fluid loss
Note: Consult Radio Medical immediately

4.2 Acrivastine - Capsules 8mg
Indication: Allergy, hay fever, urticaria, allergic face oedema, allergic asthma
Dosage: 8mg when required, max 3 times daily
Note: Consult Radio Medical

4.3 Hydrocortisone - Injection 100mg
Indication: Allergy, allergic asthma, allergic face oedema
Dosage: 100mg intramuscular or intravenous as single dose
Note: Does not take effect until 4-6 hours after injection - other treatment needed in the meantime

4.6 Prednisolone - Tablets 25mg
Indication: Allergic reactions, swellings
Dosage: As prescribed by Radio Medical
Note: Consult Radio Medical

4.7 Cyclizine - Tablets 50mg (SEDATIVE)
Indication: Seasickness
Dosage: 50mg 1-2 hours before needed, max 3 doses per 24 hours

=== GROUP 5 - ANTIDOTES ===
5.1 Naloxone - Injection 0.4mg/ml, ampoules 1ml
Indication: Morphine overdose antidote
Dosage: 0.8mg (2 ampoules) intramuscular immediately, repeat after 5 minutes if no effect
Note: Consult Radio Medical

5.2 Glucagon - Injection 1mg
Indication: Insulin shock, hypoglycaemic coma, low blood sugar
Dosage: 1mg intramuscular, may repeat after 10-15 minutes
When conscious: give bread and sweet juice
Note: Consult Radio Medical

5.3 Atropine - Injection 1mg/ml
Indication: Poisoning with insecticides containing organophosphates or carbamates
Dosage: 2mg intravenous slowly, repeat every 5-10 minutes until pupils dilate
Note: Consult Radio Medical - MFAG table 17

5.6 Activated Carbon - 50g granulate
Indication: Poisoning and caustic burns in stomach and intestines
Dosage: 50-100g mixed in water as quickly as possible
Note: Do NOT use on unconscious patients

=== GROUP 6 - MENTAL DISORDERS AND CONVULSIONS ===
6.2 Diazepam - Tablets 5mg (SEDATIVE)
Indication: Anxiety, alcohol withdrawal, convulsion prevention, fractures, acute mental illness
Dosage: 5mg three times daily. Acute mental illness: dose per Radio Medical
Note: Consult Radio Medical - addictive

6.3 Diazepam - Enema 4mg/ml (SEDATIVE)
Indication: Universal convulsions, epilepsy, acute mental illness
Dosage: Per Radio Medical prescription
Note: Consult Radio Medical - takes effect 5-10 minutes after rectal administration

6.4 Haloperidol - Injection 5mg/ml (SEDATIVE)
Indication: Acute mental illness with mania, delusions, hallucinations, nausea, vomiting
Dosage: 5-10mg intramuscular, may repeat 3 times daily
Note: Consult Radio Medical

6.5 Zolpidem - Tablets 10mg (SEDATIVE)
Indication: Insomnia, difficulty falling asleep
Dosage: 10mg 15-30 minutes before bedtime

=== GROUP 7 - INFECTIONS ===
7.1 Phenoxymethylpenicillin - Tablets 660-800mg
Indication: Throat infection, oral infections, sinusitis, middle ear inflammation, pneumonia
Dosage: 400-800mg 2-4 times daily
Note: Consult Radio Medical. Allergic shock possible within 30 min - treat with Adrenaline (4.1)

7.2 Amoxicillin - Capsules/tablets 500mg
Indication: Pneumonia after chemical inhalation
Dosage: 375-750mg twice daily
Note: Consult Radio Medical

7.6 Metronidazole - Tablets 500mg
Indication: Throat abscess, tooth abscess, bone infection, abdominal infections, appendicitis
Dosage: 500mg three times daily
Note: Consult Radio Medical. Often combined with Ciprofloxacin (7.8) or Ceftriaxone (7.9)

7.8 Ciprofloxacin - Tablets 250mg
Indication: Tooth abscess, severe bronchitis, acute diarrhoea, appendicitis, urinary infection
Dosage: 250-750mg twice daily
Note: Consult Radio Medical. Often combined with Metronidazole (7.6). Not for pregnant patients

7.9 Ceftriaxone - Injection 1g
Indication: Sepsis, blood poisoning, meningitis, pneumonia, appendicitis, major wounds, open fractures
Dosage: 1-2g intravenous daily as single dose
Note: Consult Radio Medical. Often combined with Metronidazole (7.6) - MFAG table 10

7.10 Tetanus Vaccine - Injection
Indication: Accidents with wounds unless patient is covered by tetanus vaccine
Note: Consult Radio Medical

=== GROUP 8 - EYE MEDICINE ===
8.1 Chloramphenicol - Eye drops/ointment
Indication: Eye infections, bacterial conjunctivitis
Note: Consult Radio Medical

=== GROUP 9 - CARDIOVASCULAR ===
9.1 Glyceryl Trinitrate (Nitroglycerin) - Spray
Indication: Angina pectoris, chest pain suspected cardiac
Dosage: 1-2 sprays under tongue, may repeat after 5 minutes
Note: Consult Radio Medical immediately for chest pain

9.9 Tranexamic Acid - Injection 100mg/ml, 10ml ampoules
Indication: Severe bleeding, haemorrhage control
Dosage: 1g (10ml) intravenous as soon as possible
Note: Consult Radio Medical - use within 3 hours of injury for maximum effect

=== GROUP 10 - GASTROINTESTINAL ===
10.1 Omeprazole - Capsules 20mg
Indication: Stomach ulcer, heartburn, gastric acid
Dosage: 20-40mg once daily
Note: Consult Radio Medical

10.3 Metoclopramide - Injection/tablets
Indication: Nausea, vomiting
Note: Consult Radio Medical

=== GROUP 15 - RESPIRATORY ===
15.1 Salbutamol - Inhaler
Indication: Asthma, bronchospasm, breathing difficulties
Dosage: 2-4 puffs when needed
Note: Consult Radio Medical

=== GROUP 16 - INFUSION FLUIDS ===
16.1 Sodium Chloride 0.9% (NaCl) - Infusion fluid
Indication: Shock, dehydration, fluid replacement
Dosage: 60 drops per minute via IV cannula (E.4)
Note: Start immediately in shock cases

=== MEDICAL EQUIPMENT ===
A.1 Mobile Resuscitation Kit - oxygen mask, ventilation bag
E.4 Venous Cannula (IV needle) - for intravenous access
J.1 Cold pack - for injuries, swelling
J.2/J.3 Support bandage - for sprains, fractures immobilization

=== HOW TO REFERENCE MEDICINES ===
Always use the group number when referring to medicines:
Example: "Give 3.1 Paracetamol 1g (2 tablets)"
Example: "Apply 9.9 Tranexamic Acid 1g (10ml) intravenously"
Example: "Administer 3.4 Morphine 10mg (1ml) intramuscularly"
